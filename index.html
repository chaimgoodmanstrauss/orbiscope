The pattern folds up, tile onto tile, into this surface. Hover a part to find it in the pattern and in the signature.

What the characters mean
o
a handle: the pattern repeats by sliding, and folds up into a torus-like tube.
3
a gyration point written before any ∗: the pattern turns a third of the way round it, with no mirror through it.
∗
a kaleidoscope: a family of mirror lines. Folded up, each ∗ is a hole with a mirror rim.
∗2
a digit after ∗ is a corner, where that many mirrors cross.
x
a crosscap, or miracle: a glide or turn-flip that no mirror explains.
The Magic Theorem

Each character costs something: o costs $2; ∗ and x cost $1; a gyration point of order n costs (n−1)/n; a corner costs half that. Every pattern of the flat plane costs exactly $2. Under $2 the pattern lives on a sphere, and it has 2 ÷ (2 − price) symmetries; over $2 it lives in the hyperbolic plane. Four cheap signatures — N, MN, ∗N, ∗MN with M ≠ N — belong to no pattern at all.

What you can drag

Hover the pattern: edges, cones and corners light up, and so does their character in the signature. In the hyperbolic plane, edges with a length glow when hovered: drag to stretch, shift-drag to twist, or use the wheel. Click a cone or corner to raise its number (alt-click lowers it). Flat lattices have round handles. Drag empty space to move the pattern, wheel or pinch to zoom, 0 to reset.
